## Context

The DSM-5 is the reference manual that defines mental disorders, and each
diagnosis is established through a list of criteria: the symptoms a person
must present in order to receive that diagnosis.

Looking at these symptoms, we might assume they are unique to each disorder.

Symptoms can sometimes overlap between different disorders, including
disorders that belong to different chapters.

Quantifying that overlap is the subject of a 2023 study published in
*Psychological Medicine*, which broke the manual's diagnostic criteria down
into their constituent symptoms and measured how many recur, both within
chapters and across them.

This project aims to replicate those descriptive results and turn them into
an interactive explorer: which symptoms are the least specific, which
diagnoses overlap most with one another, and which chapters share more ground
than their separation suggests. This is a declared replication, with the
published figures fixed in advance as verification criteria for the data
processing.

It is in no way intended to produce a diagnostic or screening tool. That
requires the supervision and judgement of a professional, who conducts a
clinical interview, takes a history, rules out medical causes and assesses
functional impairment.
